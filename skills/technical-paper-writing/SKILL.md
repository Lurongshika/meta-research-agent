# Technical Paper Writing

Write from verified evidence and the paper's actual contribution. Never fabricate experiments, theorem status, citations, reviews, or implementation details.

## Build the argument

Express the contribution in one falsifiable sentence. Align the abstract, introduction, method, results, and conclusion around that claim. Use related work to locate the contribution, not to produce a chronology. Distinguish what is new from what is adopted or combined.

For empirical papers, connect every headline claim to a table, figure, or analysis produced from actual results. State datasets, splits, baselines, tuning, metrics, uncertainty, compute, and limitations. For systems papers, include environment, workload, scale, overhead, and failure behavior. For mathematical papers, define notation before use, state assumptions with the theorem, and keep proof dependencies explicit.

## Calibrate language

Match strength of wording to evidence: “demonstrates” requires direct support; “suggests” is appropriate for limited evidence. Do not generalize beyond evaluated distributions, scales, or assumptions. Separate observations, explanations, hypotheses, and causal claims.

## Review and revision

Check contribution clarity, internal consistency, symbol reuse, units, table comparability, caption self-containment, citation support, reproducibility, and limitations. When responding to reviews, quote or summarize each concern accurately, state the change and its location, and do not claim new experiments completed until their outputs are verified.

## Deliverables

Return venue-appropriate prose or a structured revision, plus a claim-to-evidence audit identifying unsupported statements, missing controls, ambiguous notation, and unresolved limitations.
